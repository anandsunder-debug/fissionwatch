# Matchstick Rocket Digital-Twin Case Study

## Purpose

This case study documents a video-based engineering workflow for reconstructing a small matchstick-based object and comparing visual observations with conceptual CAD, fluid-flow, thermal, structural, and motion models.

## Workflow

1. **Observe** — inspect source video frames and identify visible components.
2. **Calibrate** — use the visible ruler or known reference dimensions where available.
3. **Reconstruct** — describe the geometry in CAD-style terms and record assumptions.
4. **Model** — define the physical questions and appropriate model classes.
5. **Analyze** — interpret illustrative CFD, thermal, structural, and trajectory graphics.
6. **Compare** — distinguish video observations from simulated or expected values.
7. **Document uncertainty** — record what is measured, assumed, inferred, or unverified.

## Source material represented

The supplied visual material includes:

- Video frame sequences showing ignition and motion.
- CAD-style and exploded-view renderings.
- Dimensioned and calibrated geometry illustrations.
- Conceptual cross-sections and mesh visualizations.
- Temperature, pressure, velocity, and streamline graphics.
- Structural stress visualization.
- Trajectory and motion-tracking plots.
- Suggested instrumentation and validation concepts.

## Interpretation rule

The visual posters are treated as documentation of the prior analysis, not as proof that every displayed number came from a reproducible solver or physical test. Reproducibility requires source video, calibration details, geometry files, material properties, boundary conditions, mesh/convergence records, solver settings, and raw experimental data.

## Scope and safety

This is an educational documentation project. It does not establish a safe design, safe operating envelope, or validated performance for a combustion-driven propulsion device. Construction, pressurization, propulsion optimization, and live testing are outside this repository's scope.
